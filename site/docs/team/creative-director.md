# The Unusual Open House: creative direction

Denise Frank / Hometown Realtors of Texas LLC. CD brief, 2026-10-06. Dossier facts only.

## 1. The one idea

You do not read about Denise. You walk through her open house at night, room by room, and leave holding a keyring you built yourself, with one hook left empty for the key she hands you in person.

Name: **The Unusual Open House**
Line: a six-minute night walk through a house in the Houston-north pines; every room teaches one lesson and hands you one key.

## 2. The arc

| # | Chapter, time | Beat | Trait | Lesson | Key, earned by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Porch, 20s | Curiosity | Prankster | Do a thing, get a key | Porch Key: knock |
| 2 | Front Hall, 40s | Relief | Houston girl, Aggie, IT brain | Buy, sell, rent, own a rental: your pick shapes the house | Hall Key: choose |
| 3 | Lab, 90s | Agency | Science nerd | True monthly cost of owning beside rent; "not yet" is a plan | Lab Key: run the numbers |
| 4 | Evidence Board, 75s | Laughter | True crime fan | Read listings like case files | Case Key: close the case |
| 5 | Library, 60s | Competence | Bookworm | Five words on your paperwork | Library Key: flip all five |
| 6 | Map Room, 45s | Belonging | Local, Aggie | Flood, MUD, commute: ask before you look | Map Key: drop a pin |
| 7 | Backyard, 45s | Affection | Animal lover | Read before you sign; never skip the inspection | Backyard Key: flip both pets |
| 8 | Closing Table | Resolve | The broker who closes | The last key is not on a website | Empty hook: House Key, in person |

Nothing cut or merged: eight rooms, eight verbs. Order is fixed: the Hall choice feeds the Lab and the finale; the Backyard is the warmest beat before the ask. Skip is always visible and lands on the same table.

## 3. Signature moments

**A. The Porch (WebGL opening).** Gravel drive in the pines at night: three parallax pine layers, ground fog, one house card, one porch light (additive sprite, halation 0.1). About 400 instanced fireflies drift on noise and lean toward the cursor (parallax 0.2). Title on a wooden sign by the mailbox, badge in cream linework, heart red. One button: Knock. On Knock: knock sound, crickets up, porch light brightens, door opens, camera dollies up the walk into the Hall (2.5s).

**B. The Evidence Board.** Corkboard under a warm lamp, a listing paragraph on an index card. Tap a word hiding something: a pin pushes in (sound) and a cream Polaroid with the plain meaning swings in on a red string pulled taut from the word. Five finds and a rubber stamp hits CASE CLOSED. Wrong taps wobble, no penalty; after 20s idle one word underlines in coral.

**C. The Closing Table (finale).** The keyring HUD slides from the corner to center and drops onto a wooden table, one clink per key, each named as it lands. The camera pulls back out of the house; every room's lamp comes on in the order you played them, fireflies back. On the table: seven keys, one empty hook tagged HOUSE KEY, HANDED OVER IN PERSON, and the actions as objects: phone, calendar, note card, photo strip. Skipped rooms show as outlined keys. Reduced motion: one crossfade.

## 4. Copy

**Hero (Porch).**
Headline: Not your usual open house.
Sub: Denise Frank, Broker and Realtor®, Houston-north. Every room teaches you one thing and hands you one key. About six minutes. Knock when you're ready.
Button: Knock
Toast: Porch Key. You knocked. Most people walk right in.

**The Front Hall.**
Hi, I'm Denise. Broker, Realtor, Houston girl, Aggie, IT before this. My girls come first. A cat and a lizard run the office. I'm not your usual Realtor, and I'd rather show you than tell you. What brought you by tonight?
Buttons: I want to buy / I want to sell / I need to rent / I own a rental
Toast: Hall Key. Now the house knows why you came.

**The Lab.**
Science nerd, reporting for duty. Rent is not "throwing money away," and buying is not always the smart move. It's math. Move the sliders and see the true monthly cost of owning, taxes, insurance, HOA and PMI included, next to rent. (No default rate is quoted.)
If the numbers say not yet: Not yet is a plan. That's Credit to Keys: rent now, build credit, buy later, on purpose.
Plaque: Estimates for learning only, not financial advice.
Toast: Lab Key. You ran the numbers before you fell in love. Rare.

**The Evidence Board.**
True crime fan. Occupational hazard: I read listings like case files. Tap every word that's hiding something. The red string does the rest.
The five: Cozy (Small. Bring a tape measure.) / Needs TLC (Needs a contractor.) / Motivated seller (Price is a conversation.) / Investor special (Bring a flashlight.) / Partial lake view (Stand on the roof.)
Toast: Case Key. You can't unsee "cozy" now. Sorry.

**The Library.**
Bookworm. Real estate has its own language and nobody hands you the glossary. Flip five cards.
MUD: a district tax for water, sewer and drainage, common in newer subdivisions. Ask for the rate.
PMI: mortgage insurance that rides along with smaller down payments. Protects the lender, not you.
Homestead exemption: a filing that can lower the taxable value of the home you live in. Easy to miss.
Escrow: where the money waits until everyone keeps their promises.
Earnest money: the deposit that says "I mean it."
Toast: Library Key. You now speak fluent closing table.

**The Map Room.**
I grew up in Houston and I work the north side. Drop a pin on your town and I'll tell you the three questions I ask first out there.
Towns: Montgomery, Magnolia, Conroe, Spring, The Woodlands, Pinehurst, Shenandoah
Field note, same for every town (Fair Housing by design): Flood zone: check the map before the paint. MUD: new street, ask the rate. Commute: drive it at 7:40 on a Tuesday.
Off-map maroon pin: College Station. Not in the service area. Whhhooop anyway.
Toast: Map Key. Know what to ask before you know where to look.

**The Backyard.**
Meet the supervisors. Rico, bearded dragon, Head of Security. Cheeto, cat, Chief Inspection Officer. Flip each between real life and real estate life, then take their advice.
Rico: Once mistook a finger for a blueberry. Now fed with tongs. / Read it before you sign it. Not everything that looks like a blueberry is one.
Cheeto: Tests every sunny windowsill before a listing goes live. / Never skip the inspection. He does windowsills. Hire a human for the roof.
Toast: Backyard Key. Both supervisors signed off. Cheeto took his time.

**The Closing Table.**
Headline: Let's get you keys.
Sub: Seven on the ring. One hook empty. That one is the house key, and I hand it over in person.
Skip route: Straight to the table. I respect that. Everything you need is right here.
Partial route: You left a few keys behind. The door still opens.
CTAs: Call Denise, (832) 928-3019 / Book a time on HAR / Send a message / Search homes on HAR / Facebook, Instagram, X

**HUD.**
Progress: Keys 3 of 7 (ring icon, empty hook always drawn)
Skip: Skip to the closing table
Sound: Sound on / Sound off. First-time hint: Crickets included.
Motion: Calm mode on / Calm mode off

**Achievements.**
Knocked First: Most people walk right in.
Straight to Business: Skipped to the table. No judgment.
Not Yet Is a Plan: Found the Credit to Keys path.
Case Closed: Found every hidden word.
Whhhooop: Found the pin that isn't on the map.
Tong Certified: Met Rico, kept all ten fingers.
Full Ring: Seven keys, one empty hook. You know what to do.

**Fallbacks.**
WebGL off: The fireflies took the night off. The house is still open. Come on in.
Calm mode or reduced motion: Same house, fewer fireflies.
Audio blocked: Your browser kept the crickets quiet. Tap the speaker when you want them.
404: Wrong door. The house is this way. Links: the Porch / the closing table.

## 5. Art direction

1. Night in the Houston-north pines: ground #072018, sky one shade lighter, never blue-black; three pine depths, base fog; few warm lights (porch, fireflies, one lamp per room).
2. Rooms are lit islands in one dark: #0d2a20 room, #133a2c props, #1f4a39 lines. No light mode.
3. Gold #d4a843 is what you can act on or have earned: keys, CTAs, titles, HUD ring. Light gold #e8c874: Lab numbers only.
4. Never: pure white (cream #f4ebdd is text); green #4cc38a outside good news; coral #e36a55 outside caution, never decoration; blue #7fb2d0 outside tips; orange #e08a4a outside hot or just listed; a recolored heart (#e12d32 always); an em dash.
5. One film grade over WebGL and photos: grain 0.08, halation 0.1, vignette 0.15.
6. Rico and Cheeto pairs: cream-bordered Polaroids, slight tilt, pinned; "In Real Life" front, "Real Estate Life" on the flip; suit, briefcase and SUV in frame; no color-shifting filters; mono caption.
7. Keys: seven distinct real shapes, cream linework, gold once earned; the empty hook always visible.
8. Type: DM Serif Display titles, large and calm, no all-caps; DM Sans body; JetBrains Mono for labels and values. Attitude: a hand-painted porch sign that opened a case file.
9. Motion: tempo 1.0, exuberance 0.3, overshoot 0.2, stagger 0.5, spring reveals; one thing moves at a time.
10. Sound: crickets, knock, pin push, string twang, key clink, lamp click; no music under copy; nothing before the knock.

## 6. Viewer test

She shares it because it is her and only her: the lizard with the tongs, the red string, the Aggie pin off the map, and every room hands a nervous first-time buyer something to use.
She cringes at "dream home," "luxury lifestyle," a stock family, a fake review or invented number, a promised rate, a neighborhood called "safe" or "great for families," a recolored heart, an em dash, Rico made cute instead of Head of Security.
Build it so she reads every line out loud and nods.
